MATURE CHRISTIAN DATING

Replit Design — Next-Generation Creative Experience Build Brief

1. YOUR ROLE

Act as a senior:

* digital product designer;
* dating-product UX designer;
* editorial art director;
* interaction designer;
* motion designer;
* conversion designer.

You are designing a new UK Christian dating product called:

Mature Christian Dating

Primary domain:

MatureChristianDating.co.uk

This is not a request for a generic dating-app landing page.

It is not a request to copy Hinge, Bumble, Christian Connection, SALT or OurTime.

The objective is to create a distinctive, contemporary digital dating experience for Christian adults approximately 40–70 years old who are looking for meaningful relationships, companionship and marriage/remarriage where desired.

The site should immediately feel:

2. IMPORTANT — USE REPLIT DESIGN CANVAS FIRST

Do not immediately build a full-stack application.

Start in the Design Canvas.

Create multiple visual directions and responsive design frames first.

The purpose of this stage is to establish:

* art direction;
* typography;
* photography;
* composition;
* motion;
* profile presentation;
* compatibility visualisation;
* founding-member onboarding;
* logged-in product concepts.

Do not build databases, authentication, payments or production matchmaking logic yet.

Those will later be implemented after the design has been reviewed.

⸻

3. FIRST OUTPUT — THREE GENUINELY DIFFERENT DIRECTIONS

Produce three substantially different visual concepts.

Do not simply change colours.

Each concept should have a different:

* hero composition;
* photographic treatment;
* typography;
* page rhythm;
* profile presentation;
* compatibility visual language;
* motion philosophy.

All three should still satisfy the core brand principles.

Create desktop and mobile frames for each.

After creating the three concepts, provide one recommended hybrid direction combining the strongest ideas.

Do not automatically convert anything to an application until the preferred direction has been reviewed.

⸻

4. BUSINESS PROPOSITION

Mature Christian Dating is a UK-wide dating platform primarily for Christian singles approximately 40–70.

It is for people seeking:

* meaningful relationships;
* long-term relationships;
* companionship;
* remarriage or marriage where desired;
* genuine connections with people whose Christian faith and values are compatible.

The audience may include people who are:

* divorced;
* widowed;
* never married;
* returning to dating after many years;
* uncomfortable with swipe culture;
* tired of casual dating apps;
* looking for people in a similar stage of life.

The product should not imply that every member wants marriage.

Life stage and relationship intentions should be explicitly represented.

The strongest underlying idea is:

6. FOUNDING MEMBER LAUNCH

The initial commercial launch is not a normal empty dating marketplace.

The first product is a:

FOUNDING MEMBER COMMUNITY

Primary CTA:

The proposition:

Join free. Tell us about yourself and who you’re hoping to meet. As our founding community grows, we’ll look for promising mutual connections. If we identify one, we’ll invite both people to consider an introduction.

Important:

Do not claim guaranteed compatibility.

Do not fabricate member counts.

Do not fabricate success stories.

Do not fabricate active users.

Do not show fake live activity.

13. PHOTOGRAPHY IS CRITICAL

Photography should dominate more than 3D.

Use an editorial photographic style featuring believable adults approximately 40–70.

Photography should feel:

* natural;
* contemporary;
* British/European;
* warm;
* confident;
* sophisticated;
* individual rather than staged couples.

Avoid:

* clichéd older couples walking on beaches;
* people laughing unnaturally over coffee;
* stock-photo handholding;
* exaggerated wedding imagery;
* teenagers/20-somethings;
* people who all look like fashion models;
* church-stock-photo clichés.

The site should visually communicate:

My life is already full. I am looking for someone meaningful to share the next part with.

Any generated imagery used during design is illustrative only and must not be presented as genuine members.

14. CHRISTIAN IDENTITY

Christianity should be unmistakable but sophisticated.

Do not cover the interface with:

Faith should primarily appear through:

A cross may appear within the brand identity if exceptionally restrained.

15. TYPOGRAPHY

Typography should become a major part of the brand.

Explore a pairing such as:

editorial serif/display face

clean contemporary sans serif

The serif should provide:

* maturity;
* humanity;
* editorial quality;
* warmth.

The sans serif should provide:

* product clarity;
* forms;
* profile information;
* accessibility.

Avoid default AI-site combinations.

Do not automatically use Inter for everything.

Do not use overly decorative wedding typography.

Large editorial statements should create visual identity without relying on effects.

⸻

16. COLOUR DIRECTION

Avoid the obvious dating palettes:

* bright red;
* hot pink;
* Tinder gradients;
* Valentine’s palettes.

Avoid stereotypical Christian:

* church purple;
* royal blue + gold;
* cream-and-cross branding.

Explore sophisticated palettes such as:

* warm mineral neutrals;
* soft stone;
* deep burgundy/plum accents;

* warm ivory;
* restrained metallic warmth.

The palette should feel contemporary in 2026 and appropriate for both men and women.

Do not make it feminine-only.

17. THE SITE SHOULD HAVE DEPTH WITHOUT LOOKING “3D”

This is one project where heavy WebGL is probably inappropriate.

Use sophisticated depth through:

* layered photography;
* subtle parallax;
* overlapping editorial layouts;
* depth-of-field treatment;
* image masks;
* slow perspective transforms;
* profile panels moving between foreground and background;
* subtle CSS 3D;
* spatial transitions.

Explore a small amount of real 3D only if it materially improves the experience.

Do not use:

* floating spheres;
* chrome objects;
* abstract 3D blobs;
* particle clouds;
* glowing portals;
* rotating hearts.

⸻

18. MOTION LANGUAGE

Motion should feel:

slow

confident

considered

human

rather than energetic.

Potential motion:

FORM INTERACTIONS

Avoid:

* bouncing;
* confetti;
* rapid micro-animation;
* dating-app heart explosions;
* gamification;
* excessive scroll hijacking.

69. SUCCESS TESTS

The selected design must pass these tests.

TEST 1 — AGE

Could a sophisticated 42-year-old use this without feeling they joined a pensioners’ dating site?

TEST 2 — MATURITY

Could a 67-year-old use this comfortably without feeling the product was designed for 25-year-olds?

TEST 3 — FAITH

Is Christianity clearly meaningful without turning the site into religious clip art?

TEST 4 — HUMANITY

Do people feel more important than technology?

TEST 5 — DIFFERENTIATION

Does it look materially different from ordinary swipe dating?

TEST 6 — TRUST

Would somebody feel comfortable giving this service personal dating information?

TEST 7 — FOUNDING MEMBER

Does joining now feel worthwhile even before a full dating marketplace exists?

TEST 8 — PRODUCT EXTENSION

Can the design evolve naturally into the full dating platform later?

TEST 9 — SEO

Can substantive search content exist within the visual system?

TEST 10 — DISTINCTIVENESS

Does the finished experience look intentionally art-directed rather than AI-generated?

The objective is not:

Make Christian dating look technologically advanced.

The objective is:

Technology should disappear behind:
